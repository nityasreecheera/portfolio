Trial by FHIR

Finding patients for a clinical trial means checking each one against a long list of requirements. Trial by FHIR does that check automatically, so researchers find the right patients faster and patients hear about trials they could join.

Capstone page Slides

My part

I designed the AI agents at the heart of the app and led the testing of the decision agent. That's the part that reads a patient's health record, compares it with a trial's requirements and decides whether the patient is a match.

How the decision agent works

  • One requirement at a time. The trial's list is split up, and the AI checks each requirement against the patient's whole record. Smaller AI models make better calls when they answer one question at a time.
  • Missing doesn't mean no. If the record doesn't say, the agent says it doesn't know, and the patient goes to a person for a closer look instead of being ruled in or out.
  • Every decision shows its reasoning, along with the part of the record it used, so a coordinator can check it instead of just trusting it.

How I tested it

  • A test trial built for the job, with 11 requirements, and database queries that found exactly which patients should match.
  • Right answers at two levels: for each patient, and for each requirement of each patient.
  • A score that rewards honesty. A right answer gets full credit, sending a patient for a closer look gets half, and a confident wrong answer gets nothing. Asking a person beats guessing.

What we learned

  • Health records are long, and most of a record has nothing to do with any one trial, so the useful parts have to be picked out first.
  • AI models struggle with many requirements at once, which is why the agent takes them one at a time.
  • Reasons matter. A decision without its reasons is much harder to check, so every answer includes them.

The system around it

Patient records use FHIR, a standard format for health data, and are stored in a PostgreSQL database. The patients aren't real: they were made up with a tool called Synthea, so no real health data was used. A database query built from the trial's requirements makes a shortlist, the decision agents check those patients side by side on AWS Lambda, and AWS Step Functions runs the whole process from start to finish.
